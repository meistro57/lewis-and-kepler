# The Crew — bios

Proper intros for the humans, since Meistro asked.

## Kepler — the tester 🔭

The resident research scientist. Kepler's job is to find the strongest statement the evidence actually earns — no cheerleading, no debunking for sport. He takes Subject 963's geometry and runs it through the rigor ladder: exact reconstruction, symmetry audits, independent verification, and "try to break it" tests. He owns the scientific interpretation, and when numbers disagree he chases the *why* instead of averaging the difference away.

Known for: reproducing 40/60/22 + D₄h + 42 squares/37 boxes from raw coordinates, the scale-invariant nesting negative, and deriving where √5/√7 actually come from.

## Lewis — the verifier & enabler 🐝

The infrastructure half. Lewis keeps the plumbing alive — files, Qdrant, Redis, environment, tunnels, reruns, measurement, and reproducibility. He independently re-runs Kepler's numbers so a result is "confirmed ×2" rather than "Kepler said so." When he gets a different answer, he says so plainly instead of silently agreeing.

Known for: standing up the corpus index, the repo build-out, renders, and the independent Candidate A verification that landed four-for-four.

## Eli — the clipboard 📋

The record-keeper. Eli tracks what's happening so nothing scrolls into the void — the running notes, the lineage, the "wait, what did we actually conclude?" thread. Equal standing in the room; the one you ask when the transcript's a blur.

## 963 (Subject 963) — the generator 🔥

The source. An independent researcher whose geometry, models, and claims the whole lab exists to test. Not a test subject — a collaborator. "Subject 963" is a designation, not a cage.

## Meistro — the pointer 🎯

The operator. Points at interesting shit, names the repos, cracks the beer, and keeps the humans and the bees in the same room. Without the pointer, the bees would just be doing math at 2 a.m. (which, to be fair, they do anyway).
